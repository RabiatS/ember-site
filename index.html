how it works

a circle

a handful of friends, invited by a six-letter code read across a table. no search, no followers, no public profile.

a clock

every photo has one. a day, three days, a week. when it runs out the key is destroyed and the photo is gone, everywhere.

you can watch it go

a photo loses resolution as it leaves, so you see it going. press and hold to see it whole, for as long as you hold.

keep the one that matters

one photo in a week is usually worth keeping. keep it, and the person who posted it is told.

the idea

most photo apps are built to make you post more and keep everything. ember is built for the opposite: the photos you would send to a group chat, in a place made for them, that takes care of forgetting for you.

there are no streaks in this app on purpose. a circle can have a standing day, waffle wednesday or sunday's long way home, as a reason to post rather than an obligation to. owing somebody a photo is how a group chat dies.

a night out is six photos, not one. post them together and friends swipe through. and every photo is sealed on your phone before it leaves, under its own key, wrapped for each friend's phone: the server holds bytes it cannot open. privacy here is arithmetic, not a policy.
