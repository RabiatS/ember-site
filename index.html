how it works

a circle, a clock, and the one you keep.

post to a handful of people. give it a life. watch it go, and keep only what mattered.

a circle

your friends, your family, the trip, the team. a handful of people, invited by a six-letter code read across a table. no search, no followers, no public profile.

a clock

every photo has one. a day, three days, a week. when it runs out the key is destroyed and the photo is gone, everywhere.

you can watch it go

a photo loses resolution as it leaves, so you see it going. press and hold to see it whole, for as long as you hold.

keep the one that matters

one photo in a week is usually worth keeping. keep it, and the person who posted it is told.

the idea

no streaks. no likes to count. a reason to post, never an obligation.

1

a standing day

a circle can have a ritual, waffle wednesday or sunday's long way home. it is a reason to post, not a debt.

2

dumps, not singles

a night out is six photos, not one. post them together and friends swipe through, like handing over your phone.

3

nothing to maintain

no story to fill, no archive to curate, no feed to keep up with. it forgets so you do not have to.

"owing somebody a photo is how a group chat dies."which is why there are no streaks in ember, on purpose.

🔒
small rooms, sealed

privacy here is arithmetic, not a policy.

a circle is a room with a door: your friends, your family, the trip. each photo is encrypted on your phone under its own key, wrapped for each friend's phone. the server holds bytes it cannot open, and when the clock runs out the key is destroyed.

no ads, no analytics, no third-party code, no location. screenshots are reported to whoever posted, and a friend can keep a photo only if you allowed it. the whole policy, in plain words.
