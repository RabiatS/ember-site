the group chat problem

six photos, four chats, every phone keeps every copy. forever.

a night out becomes a dozen sends. each one lands in somebody's camera roll and never leaves. months later the phone is full of other people's tuesdays, and nobody remembers which chat had the good one.

ember is one place for exactly those photos, for the friends, the family and the trip, and it takes care of forgetting for you.

still social. just for your people.

post your day. post the trip. say it with your voice.

everything a story or a group chat does, without the audience and without the pile-up. what you post goes to the people you chose and lasts as long as you said.

  • your day, gone tomorrow. post a photo to your friends for a day, like a story that only they can see. it is gone by this time tomorrow, everywhere.
  • the trip, as a dump. back from a weekend? post up to fifty photos in one go, give it a week, and pick exactly who gets in: a circle, a few friends, or everyone you know.
  • talk on it. leave a voice note on a photo, reply in the thread, react, stick a sticker or a gif on it. a private reply goes only to whoever posted.
  • keep the one that matters. one tap keeps a photo for good, in your keeps and your library. the person who posted it is told, because a copy taken openly is different from one taken quietly.

how it works

a circle, a clock, and the one you keep.

post to a handful of people. give it a life. watch it go, and keep only what mattered.

a circle

your friends, your family, the trip, the team. a handful of people, invited by a six-letter code read across a table. no search, no followers, no public profile.

a clock

every photo has one. a day, three days, a week. when it runs out the key is destroyed and the photo is gone, everywhere.

you can watch it go

a photo loses resolution as it leaves, so you see it going. press and hold to see it whole, for as long as you hold.

keep the one that matters

one photo in a week is usually worth keeping. keep it, and the person who posted it is told.

you can watch it go

it does not vanish off a cliff.

across its life a photo loses resolution and lifts away into grain, so you can see it going and decide whether to keep it while there is still something to keep. press and hold to see it whole, for as long as you hold.

the big copies are let go first, which is also why a week of friends' photos costs your phone almost nothing.

your phone stays light

photos that leave take their megabytes with them.

a photo you were sent lives on your phone for its week, then the key is destroyed and the copy is cleared. nothing piles up. the profile screen keeps count of what has already gone, and what would be sitting there without ember.

the idea

no streaks. no likes to count. a reason to post, never an obligation.

1

a standing day

a circle can have a ritual, waffle wednesday or sunday's long way home. it is a reason to post, not a debt.

2

dumps, not singles

a night out is six photos, not one. post them together and friends swipe through, like handing over your phone.

3

nothing to maintain

no story to fill, no archive to curate, no feed to keep up with. it forgets so you do not have to.

"owing somebody a photo is how a group chat dies."which is why there are no streaks in ember, on purpose.

🔒
small rooms, sealed

privacy here is arithmetic, not a policy.

a circle is a room with a door: your friends, your family, the trip. each photo is encrypted on your phone under its own key, wrapped for each friend's phone. the server holds bytes it cannot open, and when the clock runs out the key is destroyed.

no ads, no analytics, no third-party code, no location. screenshots are reported to whoever posted, and a friend can keep a photo only if you allowed it. the whole policy, in plain words.
